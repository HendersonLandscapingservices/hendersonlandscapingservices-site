Winter pruning basics for small trees and shrubs

Always work safely. If you are unsure, or the work involves ladders or larger branches, please ask a professional.

1. Good candidates for winter pruning
- Many deciduous trees and shrubs once leaves have fallen.
- Overlong shoots that are clearly spoiling shape or causing obstruction.
- Damaged, crossing or rubbing branches that are easy to reach from the ground.

2. What to leave until later
- Early flowering shrubs that bloom on last year’s wood (for example many spring-flowering species).
- Plants that are borderline hardy – heavy winter pruning can stress them.
- Anything that requires climbing, chainsaws or specialist equipment.

3. Making sensible cuts
- Use sharp, clean tools and make cuts just above a bud or branch junction.
- Aim to improve structure and airflow, not simply to “take everything down”.
- Step back regularly to check shape from different angles.

If you’d like help deciding what can be safely pruned in your own garden, we can arrange a focused visit and leave you with a simple plan for each plant or area.
